I want you to redesign the adventure map based on the reference image I will provide.

### Most important interaction change

I do NOT want the child to repeatedly press "Keep Going" and then return to the map and press the next stage.

The flow should be:

1. The child completes the current stage/question.
2. Immediately show a satisfying completion animation.
3. The character should visibly move along the map/path toward the next stage.
4. The completed stage should become visually completed (for example, glow, stars, checkmark, celebration effect, etc.).
5. The next stage should unlock during this transition.
6. After the character reaches the next stage, the child can simply tap the newly unlocked stage to enter it.
7. The child should NOT need an extra "Keep Going" screen just to get back to the map.

### Make the map feel alive

The map should have interactive animations and progression.

For example:

* Character walks/runs from one location to the next.
* The path ahead can animate or light up as it unlocks.
* Completed locations can have stars, sparkles, flags, or another visual completion state.
* The newly unlocked location should have a noticeable animation/glow so the child knows where to go.
* Add small environmental animations where appropriate (moving clouds, trees, water, birds, floating elements, etc.), but keep them subtle and performant.
* When the child reaches a new stage, make it feel like they actually arrived at a new place in the adventure.

### Stage states

Clearly distinguish:

* Completed stages
* Current/unlocked stage
* Locked future stages

Locked stages should look inaccessible but still interesting enough to motivate the child.

The current stage should be visually prominent.

The completed stage should remain visibly completed when the child returns to the map.

### Navigation

When the child taps an unlocked stage, open that stage directly.

Do not add unnecessary intermediate screens between the map and the stage.

When a stage is completed, the child should naturally transition back into the adventure map with the completion animation already playing.

### Important

Do not redesign the entire application.

Only improve the adventure map and its progression interaction while keeping the existing visual identity consistent.

The final result should feel like a real children's adventure game where the child is physically progressing through a world, not simply clicking through a sequence of screens.
